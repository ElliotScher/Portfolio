## Summary

**FRC 190 2026 Codebase** is the robot control software designed for FRC Team 190's 2026 competition season, competing in the FRC game *REBUILT*. The codebase is built on the WPILib framework in Java and utilizes a modular, single-repository multi-robot pattern to support the season's three separate physical robots: **V1 (DoomSpiral)**, **V2 (Turnover)**, and **V3 (TBD)**. Utilizing AdvantageKit for telemetry logging and simulation, the codebase features advanced autonomous systems across these distinct hardware architectures, including dynamic AprilTag-based pose estimation, and automated climb execution.

For a detailed explanation of the multi-robot deployment safety verification and Gradle routing task, see the [FRC Shared Codebase Design & Execution](#/projects/FRCSharedCodebase) project page.

***

## Context: The FRC REBUILT Challenge

In the 2026 FRC game, *REBUILT* (presented by Haas), alliances compete to collect 5.91-inch diameter high-density foam balls called **FUEL** and score them into their respective **HUB** targets. In the final seconds of the match, robots must traverse the field to climb the ladder on their team's **TOWER** to secure climb ranking points.

## Robot Iterations

Throughout the 2026 season, Team 190 developed three completely separate parallel robot builds, all of which competed in official events:
* **V1 (DoomSpiral)**: First robot designed to score fuel from a limited range of positions in the alliance zone.
* **V2 (Turnover)**: Second robot featuring an improved hopper for fast fuel throughput and a turreted shooter allowing for shooting while moving.
* **V3 (TBD)**: Third robot integrating the improved hopper from V2 with a multi-fuel wide shooter for high volume throughput.

## Key Technical Features

To maximize cycle speeds and optimize autonomous scoring, the 2026 codebase features high-level automation systems that operate on top of the physical robot subsystems:

### 1. Dynamic Path Planning and Obstacle Avoidance
To traverse the field efficiently in *REBUILT*, the robot must navigate around dynamic field barriers (such as the Left and Right Bumps, and the Left and Right Trenches) depending on the active match conditions. The codebase utilizes PathPlanner's pathfinding capabilities to construct on-the-fly corrective trajectories:
* **Dynamic Obstacle Injection**: The command [AdjustPathCommand](https://github.com/Team-190/2k26-Robot-Code/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/commands/shared/AdjustPathCommand.java) updates the list of active field obstacles dynamically. Depending on the `PathAdjustmentMode` (e.g., `LEFT_BUMP`, `RIGHT_BUMP`, `LEFT_TRENCH`, `RIGHT_TRENCH`), the coordinate regions of these obstacles are flipped to match the alliance color using [AllianceFlipUtil](https://github.com/Team-190/2k26-Robot-Code/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/util/AllianceFlipUtil.java) and passed to PathPlanner via `Pathfinding.setDynamicObstacles`.
* **On-The-Fly Trajectory Generation**: As the target pose changes or obstacles update, the command interrupts the active trajectory follower, queries `AutoBuilder.pathfindToPose` to plan a new path from the current estimated robot position, and seamlessly executes the new motion profile.
* **Auto-Correction and Recovery**: If the robot is pushed or encounters defensive opponents, the closed-loop pathfinding automatically recalculates a safe path around obstacles to ensure the robot reaches the target scoring or intaking zone without driver intervention.

### 2. Auto-Aim Turret with Chinese Remainder Theorem Positioning
Turnover's shooter mechanism is mounted on a 360-degree turret to allow shooting Fuel on the fly while traversing the field:
* **CRT Absolute Encoder Positioning**: In [Turret.java](https://github.com/Team-190/2k26-Robot-Code/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/shared/turret/Turret.java), the turret computes its absolute continuous angle using the Chinese Remainder Theorem (`calculateTurretAngle`). By reading two absolute encoders geared to different tooth counts (Gear 1 and Gear 2), the system calculates the exact turret orientation without requiring homing sensors or starting calibration.
* **Field-Relative Targeting with Feedforward**: When locked on target, the turret computes its heading relative to a field translation coordinate goal using the robot's pose estimator. To shoot accurately while moving, the system calculates a feedforward velocity voltage in `calculateFeedforwardVoltage` based on the robot's current translation speeds and angular rates, correcting for chassis movement on the fly.
* **Soft Limits and Automatic Wrapping**: The turret operates within soft rotation limits. When the turret approaches its range limit, the state machine triggers an `UNWRAPPING` sequence. It computes a safe wrap-around setpoint using `Math.IEEEremainder` to quickly rotate the turret 360 degrees back into its safe operating range without tearing internal wiring.

### 3. Iterative Shoot-on-the-Move Vector Projection
To score fuel accurately while the robot is moving at high speeds, the codebase implements an iterative kinematics and vector-based prediction algorithm in [V2_TurnoverShotCalculator.java](https://github.com/Team-190/2k26-Robot-Code/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/v2_Turnover/shooter/V2_TurnoverShotCalculator.java):
* **Vector Lookahead & Time of Flight Projection**: Rather than aiming directly at the target's current relative position, the shot calculator predicts where the robot will be when the fuel ball is launched and reaches the goal. It executes an iterative convergence loop (up to 20 cycles) that computes the ball's **Time of Flight** ($t$) based on distance, projects the robot's pose forward by the velocity vector ($\Delta \vec{s} = \vec{v}_{field} \cdot t$), and recalculates distance until the error converges within 5 millimeters.
* **Chassis Velocity Feedforward Compensation**: Once the future shooting position is established, the system calculates the required turret rotational velocity ($\omega = (v_y \cdot x - v_x \cdot y) / d^2$) to counteract the robot's own linear movement, feeding this feedforward value into the turret's motor controllers so it remains perfectly locked on target while driving.
* **Setpoint IIR Filtering**: A single-pole IIR filter is applied to the raw target hood angle to smooth out setpoints, preventing rapid velocity oscillations and reducing physical stress on the shooter hood actuators.

### 4. Torque-Based Hopper Anti-Jamming System
DoomSpiral and Turnover utilize a high-throughput hopper system (`V2_TurnoverClopper`) to collect and feed high-density foam Fuel to the shooter:
* **Multi-Roller Coordination**: The hopper controls three independent motor rollers: the floor intake roller, the ball tunnel roller, and the side "balls-to-the-wall" roller. These rollers operate in coordination to quickly feed Fuel into the shooter.
* **Active Jam Detection**: In [V2_TurnoverClopper.java](https://github.com/Team-190/2k26-Robot-Code/blob/main/src/main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/v2_Turnover/clopper/V2_TurnoverClopper.java), the software monitors the torque current of the side feeding roller. If the torque current spikes above a predefined threshold (`V2_TurnoverClopperConstants.BALLS_TO_WALL_CURRENT_THRESHOLD`), indicating a Fuel jam, the system triggers a custom debounced `Trigger`.
* **Automated Clearance Routine**: When a jam is detected, the hopper automatically interrupts the feeding cycle to reverse the side roller for 1 second (`shouldReverse = true`). After the jam is cleared, the system automatically restores forward voltage, preventing mechanical binding and ensuring uninterrupted feeding during high-intensity match cycles.
